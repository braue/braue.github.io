    BNC Coaxial Port#

    Overview#

    The BNC (Bayonet Neill–Concelman) connector is a quick-connect, bayonet-style RF connector used for coaxial cabling. It is widely used in instrumentation, oscilloscopes, radios, lab equipment, GPS timing receivers, and video systems.

    Terminology#

    • BNC — Bayonet-style RF connector for coaxial cable.
    • Coax — Cable with center conductor, dielectric, shielding, and outer jacket.
    • 50 Ω / 75 Ω — Characteristic impedance types; matching required for proper signal integrity.
    • RG-58 / RG-59 / RG-6 — Common coax cable types.
    • IRIG-B — Time synchronization signal used in power systems and industrial automation.
    • Return Loss — Reflection due to impedance mismatch.
    • TDR — Time-Domain Reflectometry used for cable diagnostics.

    Practical Use-Cases#

    • Oscilloscope and signal analyzer inputs/outputs
    • GPS-disciplined clocks and timing distribution
    • IRIG-B time code distribution (power systems, substations, PMUs)
    • Function generator outputs
    • RF equipment and receivers
    • CCTV and SDI video systems
    • Legacy 10BASE2 networking (historical)

    Configuration & Implementation#

    Key Parameters#

    • Impedance:
      • 50 Ω for RF, instrumentation, IRIG-B
      • 75 Ω for video (CCTV, SDI)
    • Frequency range: up to ~4 GHz depending on connector grade
    • Cable types:
      • RG-58 (50 Ω)
      • RG-59/6 (75 Ω)
    • Termination: 50 Ω or 75 Ω termination resistors depending on system
    • Connection style: Bayonet twist-lock; plug ↔ jack

    Best Practices#

    • Always match connector and cable impedance (50 Ω or 75 Ω).
    • Keep coax runs short when transporting high-frequency or timing signals.
    • Avoid sharp bends that deform the dielectric.
    • Label coax cables when distributing IRIG-B around a substation.
    • Use strain relief on portable test equipment.
    • Avoid adapters (BNC-to-SMA, etc.) unless absolutely necessary.

    Common Pitfalls#

    • Using 75 Ω BNC on 50 Ω timing equipment.
    • Running IRIG-B through patch panels or couplers that distort the waveform.
    • Long coax runs causing amplitude droop (especially for AM IRIG-B).
    • Damaged or loose bayonet connectors leading to intermittent signals.
    • Water ingress in outdoor coax.
    • Using low-frequency coax for RF applications.

    Typical Protocols / Signals#

    BNC connectors are used to carry:

    • Analog RF signals
    • Oscilloscope probe signals
    • Clock/trigger outputs
    • Composite video
    • SDI (Serial Digital Interface)
    • GPS reference outputs (1 PPS, 10 MHz)
    • IRIG-B time synchronization signals (AM or DC)
    • Low-frequency RF
    • Substation timing distribution

    Troubleshooting & Failure Modes#

    Symptoms#

    • No signal or missing sync
    • Excessive noise or distortion
    • Bad video or timing errors
    • PMU desynchronization
    • Oscilloscope ringing on square pulses
    • Intermittent triggers

    Root Causes#

    • Impedance mismatch (50 Ω ↔ 75 Ω)
    • Damaged coax or crushed dielectric
    • Missing termination on IRIG-B or video links
    • Water or corrosion
    • Cable too long for the frequency/timing signal
    • Loose or worn bayonet connectors

    Fixes / Workarounds#

    • Replace damaged coax
    • Add or verify correct termination
    • Ensure impedance is matched end-to-end
    • Use high-quality RG-58 for IRIG-B
    • Avoid T-connectors unless required by legacy systems
    • Clean or replace corroded connectors

    Field Test Methods#

    • Continuity testers
    • TDR for impedance & distance-to-fault
    • Oscilloscope for IRIG-B waveform verification
    • Spectrum analyzer or VNA for RF
    • SDI monitors for video integrity
    • SEL/Arbiter clock status screens

    Standards & References#

    • IEC 60169-8 — BNC connector standard
    • IRIG Standard 200-04 / 200-16 — IRIG time code formats
    • IEEE C37.118 — Synchrophasor & timing requirements
    • SMPTE 259M / 292M / 424M — SDI coax video standards
    • IEEE 802.3 (10BASE2) — Historical coax Ethernet
